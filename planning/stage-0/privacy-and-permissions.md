# Privacy and permission register

## Default public-scope rule

Nothing becomes public merely because it appears in a supplied document. The source materials may be used to plan and fact-check. Publication requires a content-level decision and, for media or collaborative work, any necessary permission.

## Known conflicts or verification needs

- Public identity is confirmed as `Ray Sha`; earlier abbreviated references are superseded.
- Some timelines extend into 2026 and include `current`; these must be rechecked at publication time.
- Pesticide paper authorship and journal-review status must be confirmed against the current manuscript record.
- Organization naming varies (`Students for a Greener Future` / `Students for a Better Future`). Resolve the official name before publishing.
- Environmental-club naming varies (`E# Club` / `Environmental Club`). Decide whether one is the official public name.
- Grade/year labels and activity durations vary across source documents.
- Competition and award names include translated, local, regional, and international levels; use official wording only.
- `UN keynote` / `ECOSOC keynote` context and organizer must be verified so the site does not imply a role beyond the event.
- Numerical claims such as team size, membership, classroom count, field trials, distances, sampling points, and audience size need evidence-level review.

## Media intake decision

When images arrive later, each file is quarantined from the public directory until ownership, visible people, location metadata, caption facts, alt text, and publication scope are reviewed. A visually strong image is not automatically a safe image.

## Explicit approval needed from Ray

1. Public name and whether any school/location references may appear.
2. Which recognitions, scores, or credentials—if any—belong on the site.
3. Which unpublished papers, figures, and field data may be summarized or downloaded.
4. Whether collaborators may be named and which third-party images/quotes have permission.
5. Public contact method.
6. Whether the initial public release may include routine-life details such as cycling locations.
